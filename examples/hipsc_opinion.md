# Opinion: hiPSCs at the threshold of clinical translation

Human induced pluripotent stem cells (hiPSCs) have decisively moved from laboratory discovery toward clinical therapy. Fifteen years after the original reprogramming of somatic cells [@doi:10.1016/j.cell.2007.11.019], hiPSC-derived products are entering regulated trials for Parkinson's disease, retinal degeneration, and cancer immunotherapy [@doi:10.3389/fcell.2025.1627149]. The most compelling frontier is organoid engineering: hiPSC-derived liver organoids now support extracorporeal perfusion and regenerative transplantation [@doi:10.1002/orm2.70020], while kidney organoids enable disease modelling and preclinical repair strategies [@doi:10.1159/000541919]. In my view, the decisive bottleneck is no longer reprogramming biology but GMP-scale manufacturing, genomic stability, and immune compatibility—challenges best addressed by automation and AI-guided differentiation.
